Frequently Asked Questions
Q: Which is best for frequent flyers and long-haul travel?
A: The Sony WH-1000XM5 is the top choice for travel. Its Auto NC Optimizer automatically adjusts noise cancellation for airplane cabin pressure, delivering noticeably better ANC than competitors in-flight. The 30-hour battery easily covers long-haul routes, and the foldable design stows neatly in a carry-on. The Bose QC45 is also a strong travel companion — its lighter weight (238 g) makes it more comfortable on 10+ hour flights.
Q: Can I connect to my laptop and phone at the same time?
A: Only the Sony WH-1000XM5 supports true multipoint Bluetooth, allowing you to stay connected to two devices simultaneously — for example, your work laptop and personal iPhone. Audio automatically switches when you receive a call. The Bose QC45 and AirPods Max both require manual device switching, though AirPods Max handles this seamlessly within the Apple ecosystem.
Q: Which is most comfortable for 8-hour workdays?
A: For extended office or work-from-home use, the Bose QC45 edges ahead due to its PlushComfort ear cushions and the lightest weight (238 g) of the three. The Sony WH-1000XM5 is also comfortable, though some users note the pads may feel slightly warm after 4–5 hours. The AirPods Max, while premium, weigh 385 g which can cause fatigue during marathon sessions.
Q: Are these headphones compatible with both Android and iPhone?
A: Yes — the Sony WH-1000XM5 and Bose QC45 work equally well with Android and iOS. Both offer companion apps on both platforms for EQ customization. The AirPods Max connect to any Bluetooth device but are designed for the Apple ecosystem; features like Spatial Audio, easy cross-device switching, and Siri integration require iOS 14.3 or later.
Q: Do any of these headphones support lossless audio?
A: The Apple AirPods Max support Apple Lossless (ALAC) via the Lightning-to-3.5mm cable for a wired connection, and wirelessly through Apple Music's Lossless tier via the H1 chip's low-latency link. The Sony WH-1000XM5 supports Hi-Res Audio (LDAC codec) wirelessly on Android, enabling up to 990 kbps for near-lossless quality. The Bose QC45 does not support Hi-Res Audio codecs.
